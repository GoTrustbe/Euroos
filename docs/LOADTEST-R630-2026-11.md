# EuroOS load test on a Dell R630 under KVM (2026-11-02/03)

*Branch `feature/loadtest-r630`, started from `813e627` (`feature/app-control`). Bug entries
with reproductions: [`LOADTEST-TRACKER.md`](LOADTEST-TRACKER.md) (BUG-012 … BUG-027). Small
summary logs: [`proof/loadtest-r630-2026-11/`](proof/loadtest-r630-2026-11/).*

Status labels as elsewhere: ✅ fixed and verified by a run · 🟡 mitigated or partial · ⬜ open.

## 1. Summary

- **First KVM numbers for the J1 locks.** With 8 vCPUs pinned to 8 physical cores of one socket,
  8 cores against 1: fs x3.4, ipc x2.8, unix x3.7 (medians of 5 runs); the same 8 vCPUs spread
  over both sockets: x2.0, x1.7, x2.6. Under TCG the plan measured x3.3/x3.0/x3.4 at 4 cores; under
  KVM, unpinned, 4 cores give x1.8/x1.1/x1.6, pinned to one socket x2.5/x1.8/x2.1. Absolute
  rates at 4 cores are 6.7–9.3× the TCG rates when pinned (4–6.5× unpinned); 8 pinned cores
  reach fs 0.2 M, ipc 3.3 M, unix 4.0 M ops/s. The fs workload is bound by the shared heap (76 %
  of its acquisitions contended at 8 cores); ipc and unix show no lock contention at all and are
  bound by cache-line traffic between cores.
- **Sixteen findings: ten fixed on this branch, one mitigated, five open.** Each fix has its
  own commit and a regression check: four could halt or panic the kernel or stop every new
  process (BUG-016 processes above 1 GiB, BUG-024 spawn race, BUG-025 large-file panic, BUG-014
  scheduler slot leak), BUG-018 had made every
  inbound TCP connection impossible since August, BUG-012 made more than 8 vCPUs silently
  single-core, BUG-017 left pthread joiners blocked forever (also two boot demos). Open:
  BUG-019/026 (synchronous network waits freeze the desktop loop), BUG-020 (`[fuzz]` under KVM),
  BUG-021 (NVMe BAR above 512 GiB), BUG-027 (kernel heap grows ~33 kB/min under the soak mix),
  and the root fix of BUG-023 (an incremental scrub).
- **Soak**: a first soak hit a kernel page fault after 59 minutes (BUG-024, fixed). The second
  ran **4 h 10 min** on the image with BUG-012…016, 018, 022–024 fixed: 9 265 commands, 31 520
  processes, one failed command (a fetch, BUG-026), no crash, frames and every other counter
  flat except the kernel heap (+8.2 MB). A third soak of 2 h 20 min on the final image, in
  parallel: 0 failures (§4.6).

## 3. Lock scalability under KVM (task 1)

median of the three, every operation verified (0 correctness errors, 0 order inversions in all
runs below). One run = one boot. Five boots per configuration; the tables give the median of the
five and the range. "Scaling" is N cores / 1 core **inside the same boot**.

EuroOS uses at most 8 cores (`MAX_CPU`); `-smp 16` runs on 8 (BUG-012: before the fix it ran
the "8-core" figures on one core).

### 3.4 What these numbers show, and what not

- **Shown:** the J1 locks keep their correctness under real parallel hardware execution
  (millions of verified operations per boot, no lost update, no inversion), and with the vCPUs on
  separate physical cores of one socket they scale to x2.8–x3.8 at 8 cores, while the
  one-global-lock variant falls to x0.6–x0.7. At 8 pinned cores J1 against one lock: fs ×5.0,
  ipc ×5.5, unix ×6.6.
- **Bottlenecks, from the per-lock statistics of the same runs:** fs is bound by the shared heap
  lock: 76 % of its acquisitions contended at 8 cores (EuroFS moves 4 KiB blocks; the per-CPU
  caches only cover ≤ 2 KiB, plan §7). fs on one mount is one lock by design (99.97 % contended).
  ipc and unix have **no contended lock at all**; what limits them is memory traffic between
  cores: crossing the socket halves their scaling, and the unpinned runs (vCPUs on any host
  thread, including hyper-thread siblings and the other socket) lose most of the gain.
- **Not shown:** bare-metal numbers. A vCPU is a host thread: the host scheduler, other tenants
  (two driver teams used the host) and hyper-threading are in every number; the spread of up to
  ±25 % in the unpinned runs is that noise. The "1 core" figure itself drops with more vCPUs
  (78 k → 57 k fs ops/s from -smp 1 to -smp 8), so part of every ratio is a lower baseline; the
  host CPUs turbo higher with one busy core (up to 3.1 GHz) than with all cores busy, which
  makes the 1-core reference faster than its share (not measured separately). Only
  in-kernel workloads are measured; ring-3 processes still run on the BSP only (plan §7), so none
  of this is application throughput.

## 4. Load and endurance (task 2)

All runs: `-enable-kvm -cpu host -smp 4`, selftest image, the fixes of this branch unless said
otherwise. A command's time includes the `scon` round trip (~0.1–0.4 s).

### 4.1 Many processes and threads

`procs` scenario (`-m 4G`): waves of 64 signed musl workers started in one command, reaped by
the desktop loop, `ltstat` after each wave, then 300 workers in one command, then 16 pthread
programs at once.

| | before the respective fix | after (BUG-014/015/016/017/024) |
|---|---|---|
| workers that ran their job | 2 of 400 (the 398 with an arena above 1 GiB killed at their first instruction, BUG-016) | all; 0 isolation kills |
| scheduler slots (`tasks`) | 25 → 106 → 188 → 256, then full for good (BUG-014) | stays at its high-water mark (80–92) over every wave |
| full table | process registered with task usize::MAX, never runs, keeps 2 MiB (BUG-015) | "started 240 of 300 … stopped early", all 240 reaped |
| free frames after each wave | −80 frames per wave (unlinked PT, BUG-016) | back to the same value every wave (411 100) |
| 16 × `mpthread` at once | 16 of 16 never finish (BUG-017; with `-m 1G`, so not BUG-016) | all finish (32 at once too); live count back to its boot value |

`-m 64G` (64 792 MiB usable): boots in 178 s, the same scenario gives the same results (that run
was before the BUG-017 fix, so its pthread wave hung as above). The kernel heap stays 512 MiB
whatever the RAM.

### 4.2 Churn at scale (`-m 16G`)

`churn` scenario, scale 8, every verdict line OK and the counters back at their start values:

| | result |
|---|---|
| `churn proc 128` (glibc gtiny) | 128/128 clean exits, frames 2 981 849 → 2 981 849, demand pool, tasks, kernel stacks, file table, heap unchanged; 72 s |
| `churn thread 8` (8 × 60 cycles × 4 pthreads) | every run exit 77, sums correct, kernel stacks 0 → 0, tasks 39 → 39; 35 s |
| `churn fork 4` (4 × 6 fork/exit/wait) | every child reaped with its status, process pool unchanged; 6 s |
| `churn fs 8`, `churn net 8` | 8 × 3 × 120 files, 8 × 64 sockets: 0 failures, heap stable |

(With `-m 2G` `[churn-fork]` needs a 384 MiB arena the pool does not have: a known sizing limit.)

### 4.3 File-system I/O on a persistent disk

`fsio` scenario: blank 8 GiB disk, EuroOS installs onto it and roots there; then 16/64/256 MiB
files (`truncate` = zero-filled write, `cp`, `sha256sum`), 300 small files, a 1 GiB file request,
a manual scrub; then a reboot on the same disk and `persist-check-io`.

| 8 GiB disk, `-m 4G` | virtio-blk (legacy) | NVMe | AHCI |
|---|---|---|---|
| write 256 MiB (`truncate`) | 26.0 s ≈ 9.8 MB/s | 74.2 s ≈ 3.4 MB/s | 68.6 s ≈ 3.7 MB/s |
| copy 256 MiB (`cp`, read + write) | 50.1 s ≈ 5.1 MB/s | 88.9 s ≈ 2.9 MB/s | 112.1 s ≈ 2.3 MB/s |
| read + SHA-256 of 256 MiB | 40.2 s ≈ 6.4 MB/s | 53.4 s ≈ 4.8 MB/s | 77.3 s ≈ 3.3 MB/s |
| 300 small files, median per write | 70 ms | 70 ms | 70 ms |
| manual scrub, ~600 MiB stored | 62 s | 124 s | 134 s |
| after reboot: every file and the marker | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

NVMe needs `-cpu host,host-phys-bits=off,phys-bits=39` (BUG-021). Three VMs ran in parallel on
separate cores; the absolute MB/s carry that noise. Before the fixes: `stat` of a large file read
the whole file (BUG-022), the periodic scrub held the console for 38 s to over a minute
(BUG-023), and `cp` of 256 MiB panicked the kernel on AHCI (BUG-025). The 1 GiB request is now
refused with `NoSpace (… do not fit in memory; file left unchanged)`.

**Bottleneck:** throughput is 3–10 MB/s, of the same order on three different drivers, so the
cost sits above the drivers. Every file operation moves the whole file through one heap buffer,
computes or verifies XXH3 over all of it, and reads it block by block (`EuroFs::read_data`: one
synchronous 4 KiB request per block); during the swap self-test the BSP was sampled spinning in
`virtio_blk::kick_and_wait`. Raw device throughput was not measured separately. A boot with an
installed disk takes 330–455 s to the desktop, most of it in the disk-served self-tests and the
swap self-test (`gswap`).

### 4.4 Network (e1000e, SLIRP)

`net` scenario: 200 sequential outbound HTTP connections (1000-byte answers), 50 downloads of
200 KB, then inbound connections with `scripts/loadtest-accept.py`.

| | `net.driver=kernel` | `net.driver=user` (e1000d) | `net.driver=user` + `intel-iommu,intremap=off` |
|---|---|---|---|
| outbound connection, median | 0.13 s | 0.53 s | 0.53 s |
| outbound failures | 2 fast + 1 blocked > 60 s in 163 (BUG-026) | 1 in 250 | blocked > 60 s after 106 (BUG-026) |
| 200 KB download, median | 0.20 s, ≈ 1 MB/s incl. `scon` (30 downloads, earlier run) | 7.9 s, ≈ 25 KB/s (50 downloads) | not reached (BUG-026 first) |
| inbound accept + answer (after BUG-018) | 4/4, ~7 s each | 4/4, ~37 s each | not run |
| inbound before BUG-018 | 0/3 (accept never saw a SYN) | | |

A long run of 400 sequential connections (kernel driver): 396 OK, 2 failed fast, 2 blocked the
desktop loop for 84–85 s before failing (BUG-026). With four concurrent host clients on the
background httpd: 71 answered, 62 failed, p99 5.9 s, watchdog line (BUG-019).

**Bottleneck:** the network path waits by busy-polling the NIC from the desktop loop or a
syscall with interrupts off (`SPINS` = 4 M iterations per poll). Under KVM every NIC register
read is a VM exit, so one lost segment costs tens of seconds, and inbound connections are served
one at a time inside that loop. One NIC model (e1000e) through SLIRP, so these are latencies of
the stack, not link throughput.

### 4.5 Memory

`-m 16G` (soak, churn), `-m 64G` (procs) boot and run; frame counts return to their exact
values after process waves and churn. The kernel heap is fixed at 512 MiB: whole-file buffers
bound the largest file that can be read (BUG-025). Over the 4-hour soak the heap grew by
8.2 MB (147.4 → 155.6 MB) while frames, tasks, kernel stacks and the file table stayed flat
(§4.6, BUG-027).

Frames, tasks, kernel stacks, the file table and the process pool come back to exactly the same
values over 31 520 process lifetimes and 985 rounds of file and network work. The kernel heap
does not: it grows linearly by ~33 kB/min under this mix (BUG-027, open). A separate experiment
attributes about 40 bytes to each spawned process (240 spawns: +8.7 to +11 KB); 150 outbound
connections and 40 socket churn rounds left the heap unchanged; the rest is not attributed. At
this rate the 512 MiB heap (148 MB in use) would last days, not weeks. The longest lock holds are
the BUG-019/023/026 pattern: the root-mount lock held for seconds by the synchronous disk path
(`churn fs` on the disk root, the scrub), `BG` held up to 135 ms with interrupts off, the network
locks up to 80 ms.

## 6. Limits of this test

- **A VM is not bare metal.** vCPUs are host threads; MMIO and port I/O are VM exits (that makes
  the busy-poll paths far slower than under TCG or on hardware, which is how BUG-019/026 became
  visible); timer and TSC are virtualised. The R630's own NICs and disks were not used.
- **One NIC model, through SLIRP:** e1000e only (the in-kernel and the userspace driver), user-mode
  networking, so no link-rate throughput and no real packet loss; inbound concurrency was tested
  through hostfwd from the same host.
- **Disks are files** on the host's file system (virtio legacy, NVMe, AHCI as QEMU emulates them);
  no fault injection, no power loss, page cache of the host in between.
- **Shared host:** two driver teams used the host at the same time (VFIO, eno3/eno4); VMs were
  pinned to disjoint CPUs, but memory bandwidth and caches are shared. The pinned runs are the
  cleanest numbers; the unpinned ones vary by up to ±25 %.
- **Signing:** a throw-away key; the dev-key markers fail on this host and were not counted.
- **Scope:** ring-3 processes run on one core (plan §7), so the lock numbers are in-kernel
  workloads only; the GUI was not exercised; the soak drives the shell, not the desktop.
- **Durations:** one soak of 4 h 10 min, one of 2 h 20 min on the final image, and a first one
  stopped by BUG-024 after an hour; rare races and slow leaks beyond that window are not
  excluded (the heap growth of BUG-027 needs days to matter).
